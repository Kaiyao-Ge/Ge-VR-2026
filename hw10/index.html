AI attends where you don't

Where you look, you need no help. The AI attends to the rest.

Only about 2 degrees of your view is sharp (Strasburger et al., 2011). In the gorilla study, half of viewers missed a person in a gorilla suit (Simons & Chabris, 1999).

Glasses

The glasses are only senses and a display.

A pocket device runs small models. The cloud runs the large model.

Ideas from The Way Things Work: lenses, echo ranging, infrared.

The scenario

One afternoon, I plan our VR final project with four classmates. They are Siva, Ben, James and Kaifang. The project is a game called Lighthouse. We all wear the glasses. I assume light glasses, good eye tracking and an AI that reads a scene.

1 Desk

I look at: the desk. I ask, "Where's my USB drive?"

The AI saw: my USB drive at 22:41. I never looked at it.

Idea: what I did not look at is still in memory.

2 Street

I look at: my phone.

The AI sees: a bicycle coming from behind.

Idea: the warning grows step by step. Glow, then sound, then text, then a tap. Only real danger may draw in the middle of my view. It works with the network down.

3 Table

I look at: my laptop.

The AI hears: Kaifang asks, "Hand tracking is your part, right?"

Idea: a cue shows the sentence at the edge. If I look at Kaifang, the AI stays silent.

4 Record

I look at: Siva, my laptop, Kaifang. Never the whole board.

The AI reads: the board and the talk, from the best view. It keeps every version.

Idea: the minutes list owners and dates. Each item links to its source. "What you missed" is only mine.
